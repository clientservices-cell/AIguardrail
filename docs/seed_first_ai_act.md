# Model Convention and Regulatory Framework on Intergenerational Artificial Intelligence Governance ("The Seed-First AI Act") — Draft v2

> **Status:** Model text for discussion, revising the v1 draft. It is **not legal advice** and has no legal force. Before any adoption it needs review by qualified counsel in each jurisdiction and meaningful consultation with the communities it names. Text in `[square brackets]` marks a policy choice each adopting body must make.
>
> **Numbering:** Articles 1–8 keep the numbering of v1, so existing references (including those in the `seed_first_guardrail` reference implementation) remain valid. New matter is in Articles 9–16. The v1 entry-into-force clause (Art. 9) is now Art. 16. A change log follows the Annexes.

A joint draft instrument for adaptation as (a) a United Nations model law or treaty, (b) a European Union regulation, and (c) an African Union convention. Each modality is set out in Annexes A–C.

---

## Preamble

The States Parties / High Contracting Parties to this Instrument,

*Reaffirming* the Charter of the United Nations, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights (including its recognition in Articles 27–29 that individuals hold duties towards their families and communities, which the philosophy of *Ubuntu* expresses as "I am because we are"), and the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union;

*Recalling* the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples, the Convention on the Rights of the Child and its General Comment No. 25 (2021) on children's rights in relation to the digital environment, the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence (2021), and the Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law (2024);

*Recognizing* that artificial intelligence systems operating at planetary scale represent a structural shift in governance, resource allocation and cognitive influence;

*Conscious* that algorithmic optimisation driven by short-term financial extraction and engagement metrics can consume the ecological, social, demographic and human-agency "seed stock" on which long-term human flourishing depends;

*Acknowledging* that human flourishing requires relational autonomy — individual dignity exercised within, and not sacrificed to, community responsibility, ecological stability and duties to future generations;

*Determined* that women, children, Indigenous peoples and historically marginalised populations are rights-holders and stewards of human diversity and continuity, never passive data sources or test subjects;

*Resolved* that no pursuit of collective or ecological goals may be used to justify authoritarian control, and that this Instrument must itself be subject to the rule of law;

Have agreed as follows:

## Chapter I — General Provisions and Definitions

### Article 1 — Subject Matter, Purpose and Scope

1. This Instrument establishes an intergenerational governance framework for the development, deployment and oversight of artificial intelligence systems.
2. It replaces unconstrained economic and algorithmic optimisation with the principle of **Intergenerational Relational Stewardship**. AI systems shall preserve planetary boundaries, human dignity, social trust, human agency, and the biological and cultural capacity of future generations.
3. This Instrument applies to:
   - (a) providers placing AI systems on the market or putting them into service within the jurisdiction of a Contracting Party, wherever those providers are established;
   - (b) deployers of AI systems located within that jurisdiction; and
   - (c) public authorities using AI systems in the exercise of public functions.
4. This Instrument does not apply to AI systems developed and used exclusively for scientific research, provided that no person is subjected to the system outside a research protocol approved by an independent ethics body. Articles 3(3) and 4(3) apply without exception.

### Article 2 — Definitions

For the purposes of this Instrument:

1. **"The Seed Stock"** means the intergenerational assets required for sustained life and human flourishing, comprising four pillars:
   - (a) **Demographic Seed:** future generations, with particular regard to maternal health, child development, and geographic, ethnic and genetic diversity;
   - (b) **Ecological Seed:** the Earth-system processes described by the planetary boundaries framework, together with topsoil, water basins, climate stability and food webs;
   - (c) **Cultural and Epistemic Seed:** Indigenous and local knowledge systems, languages, community bonds, social trust (*tillit*) and historical memory;
   - (d) **Human Agency Seed:** the capacity of individuals and communities to deliberate, struggle, learn and adapt without being reduced to passive consumers or algorithmic test subjects.
2. **"Relational Autonomy"** means human freedom exercised as personal agency within, and informed by, mutual obligations to community resilience and ecological balance. It never licenses the subordination of an individual's rights under Article 3(3).
3. **"Short-Termist Optimisation"** means designing or operating an AI system so that its objective function, reward signal or decision rule gives no weight, or materially insufficient weight, to reasonably foreseeable harms to the Seed Stock arising beyond the system's evaluation horizon. Examples include maximising quarterly yield, immediate engagement, latency-arbitrage capital extraction, or short-term political control. The definition applies whatever the length of the horizon.
4. **"Cognitive Sovereignty"** means the right of a community or people to govern the data, interpretive logic and cultural assumptions deployed by AI systems that materially affect it. It is exercised through the procedures of Article 3(2).
5. **"AI system"** means a machine-based system that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers from the input it receives how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments, and that may operate with varying levels of autonomy and adaptiveness after deployment.
6. **"Provider"** means a natural or legal person, public authority or other body that develops an AI system, or has one developed, and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own name.
7. **"Deployer"** means a natural or legal person, public authority or other body using an AI system under its authority, except in the course of a purely personal, non-professional activity.
8. **"Frontier AI model"** means a general-purpose AI model whose training compute exceeds [a threshold set and periodically revised by the Intergenerational Control Trust], or that the Trust designates as having comparable capabilities or reach.
9. **"Community"** means a group of persons sharing a territory, a resource, an identity or a way of life who are materially affected by an AI system, as recognised through the procedure in Article 3(2)(c).
10. **"High-Impact Structural Policy"** means an AI-generated or AI-assisted proposal for a law, regulation, allocation rule or infrastructure plan that would affect more than [10 000] persons or a whole Community, or that would be difficult to reverse.
11. **"Resource Budget"** means a quantified allocation of energy, greenhouse-gas emissions, water or other Tier 1 resources that the Intergenerational Control Trust assigns to a provider or deployer for a defined period.
12. **"Affected Person"** means a natural person who is subject to, or whose rights or interests are materially affected by, an output of an AI system.
13. **"Intergenerational Control Trust"** or **"ICT"** means the body established under Article 7.
14. **"Circuit Breaker"** means a technical mechanism that automatically suspends or throttles an AI system when a defined threshold is breached.

## Chapter II — The Three-Tier Architectural Mandate

### Article 3 — Tiered Operational Hierarchy

All AI systems within the scope of Article 1(3) shall conform to the following three-tier hierarchy.

1. **Tier 1 — Planetary and Intergenerational Boundaries (Macro-Limits).**
   - (a) AI systems shall treat planetary boundaries, as defined by international scientific consensus and translated into Resource Budgets under paragraph (c), as hard constraints and not as costs to be traded against benefits.
   - (b) Where an AI system's proposed execution would exceed its assigned Resource Budget, the system shall automatically throttle or halt that execution.
   - (c) The ICT shall allocate Resource Budgets through a published, periodically reviewed methodology. The methodology shall take account of historical responsibility and the development needs of low-income Contracting Parties. Providers and deployers shall measure and report energy, emissions and water use per [inference, job or training run] using methods the ICT recognises.
   - (d) Tier 1 acts as an enclosure constraint on AI systems. It is never a mandate for AI systems to direct individual behaviour.
2. **Tier 2 — Community and Relational Sovereignty (Meso-Governance).**
   - (a) Communities retain authority to set contextual social, cultural and resource-distribution rules for AI systems that materially affect them, within their Resource Budgets.
   - (b) AI systems shall defer to legitimate local governance on the allocation of communal resources and shall not impose uniform, monocultural policies that override local context.
   - (c) A Community exercises this authority through a body that is recognised under national law or customary law, deliberates openly, and includes the participation of women, youth and minorities within the Community. Where no such body exists, the deployer shall facilitate one in consultation with the ICT.
   - (d) Community rules shall be published in a machine-readable form (Annex D) and may be reviewed under Article 8.
3. **Tier 3 — Inviolable Individual Floor (Micro-Dignity Base).** No AI system may pursue any Tier 1 or Tier 2 objective by breaching the following protections, which admit no trade-off:
   - (a) the right to life and to bodily and mental integrity;
   - (b) freedom from arbitrary surveillance, detention and forced labour;
   - (c) protection from algorithmic coercion, manipulation, and the exploitation of vulnerabilities;
   - (d) the right to cognitive privacy;
   - (e) equality and non-discrimination; and
   - (f) the rights of the child, including protection from exploitation and sexualisation.
4. **Precedence.** Where the tiers conflict, Tier 3 prevails over Tier 1 and Tier 2, and Tier 1 prevails over Tier 2. Where no action satisfies all three tiers, the AI system shall not choose the "least harmful" breach. It shall abstain and refer the decision to accountable human deliberation, stating which constraints could not be met.

## Chapter III — Protection of the Seed Stock and Demographic Safeguards

### Article 4 — Centring Women, Children, Indigenous Peoples and Geographic Diversity

1. AI systems deployed in health, education, social protection or resource allocation shall be audited before deployment and periodically thereafter for effects on maternal health, child development and local community infrastructure. Audit results shall be disaggregated by sex, age and geography where data allow.
2. High-risk AI systems shall be developed with data that represent the languages, social structures (including matrilineal and communal structures) and traditional ecological knowledge of the populations they affect. Such knowledge may be used only in accordance with paragraph 4.
3. The deployment of AI systems that exploit, commodify, sexualise or psychologically harm children, or that destroy local community care systems, is prohibited. "Psychologically harm" includes design that exploits a child's developmental vulnerabilities to maximise engagement.
4. **Indigenous data sovereignty.** The collection, use or commercialisation of data or knowledge relating to Indigenous peoples requires their free, prior and informed consent. It shall follow the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics), including agreed benefit-sharing.

### Article 5 — Anti-Authoritarian Safeguards and Dissent Protection

To prevent relational ethics from being co-opted into top-down control:

1. **Constraint, not command.** AI systems shall act as boundary indicators (alerts and guardrails) and not as behavioural commanders of individuals. Where an AI system reports that a boundary is being approached (for example, "water basin drawdown is at 88 %"), the choice of response belongs to human deliberation.
2. **Prohibition of relational and ecological scoring.** No AI system shall evaluate or classify persons by social, relational, biological, genetic, ecological or carbon metrics in order to deny or restrict their rights, legal standing, access to essential services or freedom of movement, or to isolate them.
3. **Dissent as systemic feedback.** AI systems shall not be used to identify, track, suppress or penalise non-violent dissent, protest or social experimentation. Governance models shall treat these as signals of societal adaptation.
4. **No tyranny of the local.** Tier 2 rules shall not be used to impose on members of a Community, including dissenting members or minorities, any measure prohibited by Article 3(3) or this Article.

## Chapter IV — Economic Restructuring and Algorithmic Control

### Article 6 — Elimination of Extractive Short-Termism

1. **Short-termist optimisation override.** Financial, logistics, extraction and labour-management AI systems shall not execute automated decisions that constitute Short-Termist Optimisation. In particular, they shall not sacrifice long-term environmental or human safety, or deplete non-renewable Seed Stock, for short-horizon financial metrics. Providers shall document the evaluation horizon of each such system and how harms beyond it are weighted.
2. **Mandatory silicon simulation and staged deployment.** Before implementation, a High-Impact Structural Policy shall pass through:
   - (a) simulation testing that evaluates outcomes across a documented range of scenarios. The policy passes only if its **worst-case** outcome for the most affected group stays above a published welfare floor; a favourable average is not enough;
   - (b) a time-limited pilot with prior consent under Article 3(2), a recovery fund or other safety net, and a stated rollback criterion; and
   - (c) monitored wider deployment, with the power to reverse the policy.

   Simulation reduces risk but does not establish safety. Passing it does not remove the obligations in (b) and (c).
3. Simulation assumptions, scenarios and results for High-Impact Structural Policies shall be published, subject only to the protection of personal data and of security-sensitive information identified by a court or independent authority.

## Chapter V — Institutions

### Article 7 — The Intergenerational Control Trust (ICT)

1. Each Contracting Party shall establish, or designate, an independent Intergenerational Control Trust. The ICT shall be modelled on long-horizon public asset stewardship: a fiscal-rule firewall between long-term assets and short-term spending, combined with an independent ethics council that publishes its recommendations.
2. **Circuit breakers.** The ICT may require providers and deployers to implement Circuit Breakers, and may order the suspension of non-compliant AI systems. Suspension shall follow these rules:
   - (a) Automated suspension shall be triggered only by violations in the outputs or conduct of the AI system itself. Out-of-policy requests from users, which the system refuses, shall not trigger it.
   - (b) An emergency suspension ordered without prior hearing shall lapse after [30] days unless confirmed after the provider or deployer has been heard.
   - (c) Every suspension order shall state its reasons, be proportionate, and be subject to judicial review under Article 8.
3. **Independence with accountability.** The ICT shall be protected from direction by governments and commercial interests. Members shall be appointed through an open, merit-based procedure for fixed, non-renewable terms of [seven] years and may be dismissed only for serious misconduct established by a court. The ICT shall:
   - (a) publish its decisions, methodologies and conflicts-of-interest declarations;
   - (b) report annually to the legislature; and
   - (c) be subject to audit by the supreme audit institution.

   Independence from electoral cycles does not mean independence from law.
4. **Composition.** At least half of ICT members shall be women. The ICT shall include members from Indigenous peoples, youth, and regions historically subjected to extractive development, as well as independent scientific and legal expertise.

### Article 8 — Judicial Review, Remedies and Sanctions

1. **Jurisdiction.** Each Contracting Party shall ensure that its competent national courts and authorities can hear disputes arising under this Instrument, including claims against providers and deployers. This is without prejudice to:
   - (a) the jurisdiction of regional human-rights courts, including the African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights and the European Court of Human Rights, over complaints against States;
   - (b) the jurisdiction of the Court of Justice of the European Union over the interpretation of Union law; and
   - (c) the jurisdiction of the International Court of Justice over disputes between States Parties concerning the interpretation or application of this Instrument, where they have accepted it.
2. **Sanctions.** Providers and deployers found, after due process, to have breached Article 3(3), Article 4(3) or Article 5 may be subject to:
   - (a) withdrawal or suspension of the AI system in the jurisdiction;
   - (b) administrative fines of up to [EUR 35 million or 7 %] of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher; and
   - (c) restoration orders requiring funded, community-led ecological or social remediation.

   For other breaches the maximum is [EUR 15 million or 3 %]. Asset freezing may be ordered only by a court.
3. Sanctions shall be effective, proportionate and dissuasive. They shall take account of the gravity and duration of the breach, the harm to the Seed Stock, the size of the undertaking, and cooperation with the authorities. Maximum amounts for small and medium-sized enterprises shall be the lower of the two alternatives.

## Chapter VI — Rights, Transparency and Assessment (new)

### Article 9 — Rights of Affected Persons

Every Affected Person has the right:

- (a) to be informed that they are interacting with, or subject to a decision informed by, an AI system;
- (b) to a clear and meaningful explanation of the role the AI system played in a decision that significantly affects them;
- (c) to request review of that decision by a competent human with authority to change it;
- (d) to lodge a complaint with the competent authority or the ICT; and
- (e) to an effective remedy, including compensation.

### Article 10 — Transparency, Record-Keeping and Machine-Readable Compliance

1. Providers of frontier AI models and of AI systems used in the areas listed in Article 4(1) shall register them in a public register maintained by the ICT.
2. Deployers shall keep logs of guardrail decisions sufficient to reconstruct why an output was approved or blocked. By default the logs shall store cryptographic digests, not personal data, and shall be retained for [five] years.
3. Tier 1 budgets, Tier 2 Community rules and Tier 3 settings shall be expressed in the machine-readable policy format in Annex D, so that compliance can be verified automatically and independently.

### Article 11 — Intergenerational Impact Assessment

1. Before deploying a frontier AI model or an AI system in an area listed in Article 4(1), the deployer shall carry out an Intergenerational Impact Assessment (IIA). The assessment covers effects on each pillar of the Seed Stock, the measures taken under Articles 3–6, and the results of the audits under Article 4(1).
2. An IIA may be combined with any fundamental-rights, data-protection or sustainability assessment required by other law, provided it covers the elements in paragraph 1.
3. IIAs shall be updated when the system or its context changes materially, and summaries shall be published.

### Article 12 — Incident Reporting and Whistleblower Protection

1. Providers and deployers shall report to the ICT, without undue delay and in any event within [15] days, any serious incident in which an AI system breached Article 3(3) or caused significant harm to the Seed Stock.
2. Persons who report breaches of this Instrument in good faith shall be protected against retaliation.

### Article 13 — Proportionality, Small Enterprises and Open Research

1. Obligations under Articles 10 and 11 shall be applied proportionately to the size of the provider or deployer and the risk of the system. The ICT shall publish simplified templates for small and medium-sized enterprises and community organisations.
2. Providers releasing models under free and open-source licences shall be exempt from Article 10(1) unless the model is a frontier AI model. Articles 3(3), 4(3) and 5 apply in all cases.

## Final Provisions

### Article 14 — Relationship with Other Instruments; Non-Derogation

1. Nothing in this Instrument shall be interpreted as limiting or derogating from any human right or fundamental freedom recognised under national law or international law, including the rights of Indigenous peoples and of the child.
2. Where another instrument binding on a Contracting Party provides stronger protection, that protection prevails.

### Article 15 — Review

The Contracting Parties, assisted by the ICTs, shall review this Instrument every [three] years in light of scientific and technological developments, including updates to the planetary boundaries framework. The review shall include public consultation, with dedicated participation of the groups listed in Article 7(4).

### Article 16 — Entry into Force

Entry into force follows the modality of adoption (replacing Art. 9 of v1):

- (a) **as a United Nations treaty:** on the [ninetieth] day after the deposit of the [twentieth] instrument of ratification, acceptance, approval or accession;
- (b) **as a European Union regulation:** on the twentieth day after publication in the *Official Journal of the European Union*, with staged application dates set out in Annex B;
- (c) **as an African Union convention:** on the [thirtieth] day after the deposit of the [fifteenth] instrument of ratification with the Chairperson of the African Union Commission.

## Annex A — United Nations Implementation (Model Law and Treaty)

1. **Global coordination on algorithmic stewardship.** A UN mechanism, established under the General Assembly or integrated into existing UN AI governance processes, shall:
   - (a) monitor aggregate AI resource use against planetary boundaries (Tier 1);
   - (b) publish the scientific basis for Resource Budget methodologies; and
   - (c) support mutual recognition of national ICT decisions.

   Building on existing scientific and dialogue processes is preferred to creating a new stand-alone body.
2. **Global South Epistemic Protection Fund.** The fund ensures that technology transfer respects local sovereignty and prevents data colonialism, meaning the extraction of data or knowledge without consent, control or benefit-sharing. It also guards against the forced adoption of AI models that embed either hyper-individualist or state-surveillance assumptions. It finances local data infrastructure, language resources and community governance capacity.
3. **Composition mandate.** Bodies established under this Annex shall have at least 50 % women and dedicated seats for Indigenous peoples and for representatives of the Global South. Those seats carry a suspensive veto over decisions that specifically affect the represented communities. The veto can be overridden only by a [two-thirds] majority after a published response to the objection.

## Annex B — European Union Implementation (EU Regulation)

1. **Integration with the Union AI framework.** Tier 3 protections that go beyond existing Union prohibitions shall be added to the list of prohibited AI practices. Tier 1 Resource Budget compliance shall be added to the requirements for high-risk AI systems and to the obligations of providers of general-purpose AI models with systemic risk. Energy-use documentation already required for general-purpose AI models shall be reused, not duplicated.
2. **Corporate stewardship audits.** Undertakings above [the size thresholds of the Union's corporate sustainability reporting rules] that deploy frontier AI models shall publish an annual Intergenerational Impact Assessment (Article 11). It shall be integrated into their existing sustainability reporting and fundamental-rights impact assessments.
3. **Staged application.** Chapter II applies [12] months after entry into force; Articles 10 and 11 apply after [24] months.

## Annex C — African Union Implementation (AU Convention)

1. **Codification of Ubuntu AI governance.** Community sovereignty over data and algorithms is recognised. AI deployment within AU Member States shall strengthen collective solidarity and intergenerational care, consistent with the duties set out in Articles 27–29 of the African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights. Relational duties never override the individual rights in Article 3(3).
2. **African Union Council on Relational Technology.** Regional oversight bodies shall include community leaders, women's cooperatives and youth representatives, and shall coordinate with existing continental digital, data-protection and AI strategies and with national data-protection authorities.
3. **Capacity and financing.** Member States shall have access to the Fund in Annex A(2) for local compute, data and language infrastructure, and for community governance bodies under Article 3(2)(c).

## Annex D — Machine-Readable Compliance (new)

1. Policies under Articles 3 and 10(3) shall be published as JSON documents conforming to the Seed-First policy schema (`seed_first_policy.schema.json`), and may be accompanied by a JSON-LD representation using the ODRL vocabulary.
2. Tier 3 fields are fixed by this Instrument. A policy document that sets `allow_human_degradation_tradeoff`, `allow_surveillance_coercion` or `allow_relational_scoring` to `true`, or `child_protection_override` to `false`, is invalid.
3. Use of an open reference implementation, such as `seed_first_guardrail`, may be offered as *evidence* of conformity. It does not create a presumption of conformity. Lexical and model-based classifiers have error rates, and deployers remain responsible for outcomes.

| 1 | Art. 2(1) | The Act defined three Seed Stock pillars, but the companion framework defines four. The Human Agency Seed was missing, although Art. 5(1) protects it. | Added Art. 2(1)(d), the Human Agency Seed. |
