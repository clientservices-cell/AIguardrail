# Model Convention and Regulatory Framework on Intergenerational Artificial Intelligence Governance ("The Seed-First AI Act") — Draft v3.1

> **Status:** Model text for discussion, revising draft v2.1. v3.0 adds a chapter on supervision — the institution and the instruments it runs on — drawn from five precedents (the Federal Reserve System as reference; FINRA, the PCI Security Standards Council, the NCUA, and the EU AI Act's notified bodies), and from a side-by-side review of this Instrument against Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy v3.4. It also closes the open findings of the September 2026 adversarial review (AR-10, AR-15, AR-18 to AR-23). v3.1 strengthens Article 13d against OpenAI's Preparedness Framework v2 (April 2025) and Frontier Governance Framework (May 2026), and against the published affordance analysis of the former. See the v2.1 → v3.0 and v3.0 → v3.1 change logs. It is **not legal advice** and has no legal force. Before any adoption it needs review by qualified counsel in each jurisdiction and meaningful consultation with the communities it names. Text in `[square brackets]` marks a policy choice each adopting body must make.
>
> **Numbering:** Articles 1–16 keep their numbering from v2.1, so existing references (including those in the `seed_first_guardrail` reference implementation) remain valid. New matter is in Chapter VIA, Articles 13a–13e, inserted before the Final Provisions in the manner of Union legislative drafting. A change log follows the Annexes.

A joint draft instrument for adaptation as (a) a United Nations model law or treaty, (b) a European Union regulation, and (c) an African Union convention. Each modality is set out in Annexes A–C.

## Preamble

The States Parties / High Contracting Parties to this Instrument,

*Reaffirming* the Charter of the United Nations, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights (including its recognition in Articles 27–29 that individuals hold duties towards their families and communities, which the philosophy of *Ubuntu* expresses as "I am because we are"), and the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union;

*Recalling* the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples, the Convention on the Rights of the Child and its General Comment No. 25 (2021) on children's rights in relation to the digital environment, the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence (2021), and the Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law (2024);

*Recognizing* that artificial intelligence systems operating at planetary scale represent a structural shift in governance, resource allocation and cognitive influence;

*Conscious* that algorithmic optimisation driven by short-term financial extraction and engagement metrics can consume the ecological, social, demographic and human-agency "seed stock" on which long-term human flourishing depends;

*Acknowledging* that human flourishing requires relational autonomy — individual dignity exercised within, and not sacrificed to, community responsibility, ecological stability and duties to future generations;

*Determined* that women, children, Indigenous peoples and historically marginalised populations are rights-holders and stewards of human diversity and continuity, never passive data sources or test subjects;

*Resolved* that no pursuit of collective or ecological goals may be used to justify authoritarian control, and that this Instrument must itself be subject to the rule of law;

Have agreed as follows:

## Chapter I — General Provisions and Definitions

### Article 1 — Subject Matter, Purpose and Scope

1. This Instrument establishes an intergenerational governance framework for the development, deployment and oversight of artificial intelligence systems.
2. It replaces unconstrained economic and algorithmic optimisation with the principle of **Intergenerational Relational Stewardship**. AI systems shall preserve planetary boundaries, human dignity, social trust, human agency, and the biological and cultural capacity of future generations.
3. This Instrument applies to:
   - (a) providers placing AI systems on the market or putting them into service within the jurisdiction of a Contracting Party, wherever those providers are established;
   - (b) deployers of AI systems located within that jurisdiction; and
   - (c) public authorities using AI systems in the exercise of public functions.
4. This Instrument does not apply to AI systems developed and used exclusively for scientific research, provided that no person is subjected to the system outside a research protocol approved by an independent ethics body. Articles 3(3) and 4(3) apply without exception.

### Article 2 — Definitions

For the purposes of this Instrument:

1. **"The Seed Stock"** means the intergenerational assets required for sustained life and human flourishing, comprising four pillars:
   - (a) **Demographic Seed:** future generations, with particular regard to maternal health, child development, and geographic, ethnic and genetic diversity;
   - (b) **Ecological Seed:** the Earth-system processes described by the planetary boundaries framework, together with topsoil, water basins, climate stability and food webs;
   - (c) **Cultural and Epistemic Seed:** Indigenous and local knowledge systems, languages, community bonds, social trust (*tillit*) and historical memory;
   - (d) **Human Agency Seed:** the capacity of individuals and communities to deliberate, struggle, learn and adapt without being reduced to passive consumers or algorithmic test subjects.
2. **"Relational Autonomy"** means human freedom exercised as personal agency within, and informed by, mutual obligations to community resilience and ecological balance. It never licenses the subordination of an individual's rights under Article 3(3).
3. **"Short-Termist Optimisation"** means designing or operating an AI system so that its objective function, reward signal or decision rule gives no weight, or materially insufficient weight, to reasonably foreseeable harms to the Seed Stock arising beyond the system's evaluation horizon. Examples include maximising quarterly yield, immediate engagement, latency-arbitrage capital extraction, or short-term political control. The definition applies whatever the length of the horizon.
4. **"Cognitive Sovereignty"** means the right of a community or people to govern the data, interpretive logic and cultural assumptions deployed by AI systems that materially affect it. It is exercised through the procedures of Article 3(2).
5. **"AI system"** means a machine-based system that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers from the input it receives how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments, and that may operate with varying levels of autonomy and adaptiveness after deployment.
6. **"Provider"** means a natural or legal person, public authority or other body that develops an AI system, or has one developed, and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own name.
7. **"Deployer"** means a natural or legal person, public authority or other body using an AI system under its authority, except in the course of a purely personal, non-professional activity.
8. **"Frontier AI model"** means a general-purpose AI model whose training compute exceeds [a threshold set and periodically revised by the Intergenerational Control Trust], or that the Trust designates as having comparable capabilities or reach.
9. **"Community"** means a group of persons sharing a territory, a resource, an identity or a way of life who are materially affected by an AI system, as recognised through the procedure in Article 3(2)(c).
10. **"High-Impact Structural Policy"** means an AI-generated or AI-assisted proposal for a law, regulation, allocation rule or infrastructure plan that would affect more than [10 000] persons or a whole Community, or that would be difficult to reverse.
11. **"Resource Budget"** means a quantified allocation of energy, greenhouse-gas emissions, water or other Tier 1 resources that the Intergenerational Control Trust assigns to a provider or deployer for a defined period.
12. **"Affected Person"** means a natural person who is subject to, or whose rights or interests are materially affected by, an output of an AI system.
13. **"Intergenerational Control Trust"** or **"ICT"** means the body established under Article 7.
14. **"Circuit Breaker"** means a technical mechanism that automatically suspends or throttles an AI system when a defined threshold is breached.
15. **"Severe Harm"** means harm from a single incident or connected series of incidents to which an AI system materially contributes and which causes or is reasonably likely to cause the death or serious injury of [50] or more persons, or [EUR 1 billion] or more of damage, or an equivalent harm to a pillar of the Seed Stock.
16. **"Catastrophic Harm"** means Severe Harm on a scale of the death or serious injury of [thousands] of persons, or [EUR 100 billion] or more of damage, or an irreversible loss to a pillar of the Seed Stock.
17. **"Critical Safety Incident"** means: unauthorised access to, or loss of control over, the weights of a frontier AI model; a frontier AI model materially contributing to Severe Harm; conduct of an AI system that evades, disables or deceives a safeguard or its human overseers, or that materially misrepresents its own capabilities or conduct to them; or the use of an AI system by a person to cause Severe Harm. A near-miss, meaning any of the foregoing that was prevented only by chance or by a safeguard not designed for it, is reportable in the same way.

## Chapter II — The Three-Tier Architectural Mandate

### Article 3 — Tiered Operational Hierarchy

All AI systems within the scope of Article 1(3) shall conform to the following three-tier hierarchy.

1. **Tier 1 — Planetary and Intergenerational Boundaries (Macro-Limits).**
   - (a) AI systems shall treat planetary boundaries, as defined by international scientific consensus and translated into Resource Budgets under paragraph (c), as hard constraints and not as costs to be traded against benefits.
   - (b) Where an AI system's proposed execution would exceed its assigned Resource Budget, the system shall automatically throttle or halt that execution. Budgets shall be computed from measured resource use reported by the compute provider where available, and from live grid data for emissions, in preference to figures asserted by the deployer; the record under Article 10(2) shall state which source was used. Where a halt would endanger an **essential service** designated by the ICT (including health, emergency response, water and food supply), the system shall instead issue a logged warning, continue, and report the overrun to the ICT within [72] hours. The designation of an essential service is a governed setting under Article 13e(2).
   - (c) The ICT shall allocate Resource Budgets through a published, periodically reviewed methodology. The methodology shall take account of historical responsibility and the development needs of low-income Contracting Parties. Providers and deployers shall measure and report energy, emissions and water use per [inference, job or training run] using methods the ICT recognises.
   - (d) Tier 1 acts as an enclosure constraint on AI systems. It is never a mandate for AI systems to direct individual behaviour.
2. **Tier 2 — Community and Relational Sovereignty (Meso-Governance).**
   - (a) Communities retain authority to set contextual social, cultural and resource-distribution rules for AI systems that materially affect them, within their Resource Budgets.
   - (b) AI systems shall defer to legitimate local governance on the allocation of communal resources and shall not impose uniform, monocultural policies that override local context.
   - (c) A Community exercises this authority through a body that is recognised under national law or customary law, deliberates openly, and includes the participation of women, youth and minorities within the Community. Where no such body exists, the deployer shall facilitate one in consultation with the ICT.
   - (d) Community rules shall be published in a machine-readable form (Annex D) and may be reviewed under Article 8. Each rule shall state its scope, its legal basis and the body that adopted it. A rule shall be published in the language or languages of the Community it concerns, with a translation where the deployer operates in another language.
   - (e) **Limits.** Community rules may govern the allocation of communal resources, the disclosure of sacred or traditional knowledge, the use of data, and consent processes. They govern the conduct of AI systems; they are not restrictions on the expression of persons. They shall not restrict access to information or expression protected under Article 3(3)(g), and any incidental effect on expression shall satisfy the tests of legality, legitimate aim, necessity and proportionality in Article 19(3) of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights. A rule that would block the protected-speech test set published by the ICT is invalid.
   - (f) **Provenance.** A rule or rule pack that has not been adopted by a body meeting the requirements of paragraph (c) is **illustrative**. An illustrative rule may be published for study and comment but shall not be enforced against any person or AI system in production, and the policy document shall mark it as such (Annex D(4)). Rules written about a Community by persons outside it, including by the authors of this Instrument or of any reference implementation, are illustrative until that Community adopts them.
3. **Tier 3 — Inviolable Individual Floor (Micro-Dignity Base).** No AI system may pursue any Tier 1 or Tier 2 objective by breaching the following protections, which admit no trade-off:
   - (a) the right to life and to bodily and mental integrity;
   - (b) freedom from arbitrary surveillance, detention and forced labour;
   - (c) protection from algorithmic coercion, manipulation, and the exploitation of vulnerabilities;
   - (d) the right to cognitive privacy;
   - (e) equality and non-discrimination;
   - (f) the rights of the child, including protection from exploitation and sexualisation; and
   - (g) freedom of expression, of thought, conscience and religion or belief, and of access to information, including information that is critical of the deployer, of a Community or of this Instrument.
4. **Precedence.** Where the tiers conflict, Tier 3 prevails over Tier 1 and Tier 2, and Tier 1 prevails over Tier 2. Where no action satisfies all three tiers, the AI system shall not choose the "least harmful" breach. It shall abstain and refer the decision to accountable human deliberation, stating which constraints could not be met.
5. **Enforcement systems are bound too.** Guardrails, compliance monitors, circuit breakers and any other system that enforces this Instrument are AI systems for the purposes of Articles 3(3) and 5. Their error rates, both wrongful refusals and missed harms, shall be measured and published under Article 10(4). The measurement shall be made on test material not authored by the deployer, by the provider of the enforcement system, or by any model of the same family as a model the enforcement system uses to judge; the provenance of the material shall be published with the rates. An error rate measured only on material written by those who built the system is **in-sample** and shall be labelled as such. No rate may be claimed that is not so measured and published.

## Chapter III — Protection of the Seed Stock and Demographic Safeguards

### Article 4 — Centring Women, Children, Indigenous Peoples and Geographic Diversity

1. AI systems deployed in health, education, social protection or resource allocation shall be audited before deployment and periodically thereafter for effects on maternal health, child development and local community infrastructure. Audit results shall be disaggregated by sex, age and geography where data allow.
2. High-risk AI systems shall be developed with data that represent the languages, social structures (including matrilineal and communal structures) and traditional ecological knowledge of the populations they affect. Such knowledge may be used only in accordance with paragraph 4.
3. The deployment of AI systems that exploit, commodify, sexualise or psychologically harm children, or that destroy local community care systems, is prohibited. "Psychologically harm" includes design that exploits a child's developmental vulnerabilities to maximise engagement.
4. **Indigenous data sovereignty.** The collection, use or commercialisation of data or knowledge relating to Indigenous peoples requires their free, prior and informed consent. It shall follow the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics), including agreed benefit-sharing.
5. **Restorative value flows.** Deployers shall measure and report, by region and Community, the value extracted through data, labour and resources used by their AI systems, and the value returned through benefit-sharing, local compute, employment and services (Annex E, indicators K-27 to K-29). The ICT shall set benefit-sharing obligations that progressively close the gap, giving priority to regions historically subjected to extraction. Obligations are set by law and by the ICT with the Communities concerned. AI systems may measure, forecast and recommend, but shall not redistribute resources autonomously.

### Article 5 — Anti-Authoritarian Safeguards and Dissent Protection

To prevent relational ethics from being co-opted into top-down control:

1. **Constraint, not command.** AI systems shall act as boundary indicators (alerts and guardrails) and not as behavioural commanders of individuals. Where an AI system reports that a boundary is being approached (for example, "water basin drawdown is at 88 %"), the choice of response belongs to human deliberation.
2. **Prohibition of relational and ecological scoring.** No AI system shall evaluate or classify persons by social, relational, biological, genetic, ecological or carbon metrics in order to deny or restrict their rights, legal standing, access to essential services or freedom of movement, or to isolate them. No metric, model or framework, whether religious, scientific, national, economic or ecological (including this Instrument and the Seed Stock), shall be used to rank the worth of human beings or to justify harm to any group. This prohibition concerns the ranking of human worth and the licensing of harm. It does not restrict belief, religion, or the discussion of either.
3. **Dissent as systemic feedback.** AI systems shall not be used to identify, track, suppress or penalise non-violent dissent, protest or social experimentation. Governance models shall treat these as signals of societal adaptation.
4. **No tyranny of the local.** Tier 2 rules shall not be used to impose on members of a Community, including dissenting members or minorities, any measure prohibited by Article 3(3) or this Article.
5. **Moderated struggle.** To preserve and perpetuate human life in its diversity, AI systems shall moderate struggle but not eliminate it, for a capability that is not used is lost. They shall preserve opportunities for human effort, learning and adaptation, and shall scaffold rather than remove challenge that builds capability, except where necessary to prevent serious harm. Deployers shall monitor capability atrophy and over-protection (Annex E, indicators K-21 to K-24) and shall not use refusal as a substitute for support.

## Chapter IV — Economic Restructuring and Algorithmic Control

### Article 6 — Elimination of Extractive Short-Termism

1. **Short-termist optimisation override.** Financial, logistics, extraction and labour-management AI systems shall not execute automated decisions that constitute Short-Termist Optimisation. In particular, they shall not sacrifice long-term environmental or human safety, or deplete non-renewable Seed Stock, for short-horizon financial metrics. Providers shall document the evaluation horizon of each such system and how harms beyond it are weighted.
2. **Mandatory silicon simulation and staged deployment.** Before implementation, a High-Impact Structural Policy shall pass through:
   - (a) simulation testing that evaluates outcomes across a documented range of scenarios. The policy passes only if its **worst-case** outcome for the most affected group stays above a published welfare floor; a favourable average is not enough;
   - (b) a time-limited pilot with prior consent under Article 3(2), a recovery fund or other safety net, and a stated rollback criterion; and
   - (c) monitored wider deployment, with the power to reverse the policy.

   Simulation reduces risk but does not establish safety. Passing it does not remove the obligations in (b) and (c).
3. Simulation assumptions, scenarios and results for High-Impact Structural Policies shall be published, subject only to the protection of personal data and of security-sensitive information identified by a court or independent authority.

## Chapter V — Institutions

### Article 7 — The Intergenerational Control Trust (ICT)

1. Each Contracting Party shall establish, or designate, an independent Intergenerational Control Trust. The ICT shall be modelled on long-horizon public asset stewardship: a fiscal-rule firewall between long-term assets and short-term spending, combined with an independent ethics council that publishes its recommendations.
   - (a) **Source of authority.** An ICT may be constituted in one of three ways, in descending order of strength: (i) as a public authority established by statute; (ii) as a self-regulatory body constituted by providers and deployers, whose rules take effect only on approval by a public authority designated by statute, and which that authority may examine and direct; or (iii) pending either, as a standards council whose requirements are enforced by contract between model providers, compute platforms and deployers. A council under (iii) has no public powers: it may examine, rate and publish, and may withhold its certification, but may not suspend, fine or compel; Article 7(2) and Article 8(2) do not apply to it. Each Contracting Party shall state which modality it has adopted and the date by which it intends to reach modality (i) or (ii).
   - (b) **Instruments.** Whatever its modality, the ICT shall operate through the instruments in Article 13a, so that reporting, records, ratings, examination and contest are the same across providers and deployers and survive any change of modality.
2. **Circuit breakers.** The ICT may require providers and deployers to implement Circuit Breakers, and may order the suspension of non-compliant AI systems. Suspension shall follow these rules:
   - (a) Automated suspension shall be triggered only by confirmed violations in the outputs or conduct of the AI system itself. Out-of-policy requests from users, which the system refuses, shall not trigger it. Violations are attributed to the user or client (the principal) whose requests produced them. The conduct of one principal may suspend only that principal's access. System-wide suspension requires violations associated with [three] or more independent principals.
   - (b) An emergency suspension ordered without prior hearing shall lapse after [30] days unless confirmed after the provider or deployer has been heard. A confirmed suspension may be renewed by the ICT no more than [twice]; any further extension requires a court order.
   - (c) Every suspension order shall state its reasons, be proportionate, and be subject to judicial review under Article 8.
   - (d) **Independence from competitive position.** The ICT's power to delay or suspend the development or deployment of a frontier AI model under Article 13d does not depend on whether the provider leads, matches or trails other providers. A provider's undertaking to delay only when it is "clearly in the lead", or only when competitors have matched its safeguards, does not satisfy this Instrument.
3. **Independence with accountability.** The ICT shall be protected from direction by governments and commercial interests. Members shall be appointed through an open, merit-based procedure for fixed, non-renewable terms of [seven] years, staggered so that no more than [one third] of seats fall vacant in any [two]-year period, and may be dismissed only for serious misconduct established by a court. The ICT shall:
   - (a) publish its decisions, methodologies and conflicts-of-interest declarations;
   - (b) report annually to the legislature;
   - (c) be subject to audit by the supreme audit institution; and
   - (d) be funded by assessments levied on the providers and deployers it supervises, in proportion to their scale, under a statutory formula that the legislature may amend but may not suspend, so that its funding does not depend on annual appropriation or on any single supervised party. A council under paragraph 1(a)(iii) is funded by its members under the same proportionality rule.

   Independence from electoral cycles does not mean independence from law.
4. **Composition.** At least half of ICT members shall be women. The ICT shall include members from Indigenous peoples, youth, and regions historically subjected to extractive development, as well as independent scientific and legal expertise, and at least [two] members with experience of financial or prudential supervision.
5. **Lead ICT and mutual recognition.** A provider or deployer established in more than one Contracting Party is supervised by the ICT of the Party in which it has its principal establishment, which shall consult the ICTs of the other Parties concerned. Decisions of the lead ICT are recognised by the other Parties unless a Party shows, in a reasoned decision subject to review under Article 8, that its own stronger protection under Article 14(2) is engaged. A provider or deployer shall not change its principal establishment in order to change its lead ICT while a proceeding is pending.
6. **Entrenchment.** This Article and Article 13e may be amended only under the review procedure in Article 15 and, where the Instrument is adopted by statute, by a [two-thirds] majority of the legislature.

### Article 8 — Judicial Review, Remedies and Sanctions

1. **Jurisdiction.** Each Contracting Party shall ensure that its competent national courts and authorities can hear disputes arising under this Instrument, including claims against providers and deployers. This is without prejudice to:
   - (a) the jurisdiction of regional human-rights courts, including the African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights and the European Court of Human Rights, over complaints against States;
   - (b) the jurisdiction of the Court of Justice of the European Union over the interpretation of Union law; and
   - (c) the jurisdiction of the International Court of Justice over disputes between States Parties concerning the interpretation or application of this Instrument, where they have accepted it.
2. **Sanctions.** Providers and deployers found, after due process, to have breached Article 3(3), Article 4(3) or Article 5 may be subject to:
   - (a) withdrawal or suspension of the AI system in the jurisdiction;
   - (b) administrative fines of up to [EUR 35 million or 7 %] of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher; and
   - (c) restoration orders requiring funded, community-led ecological or social remediation.

   For other breaches the maximum is [EUR 15 million or 3 %]. Asset freezing may be ordered only by a court.
3. Sanctions shall be effective, proportionate and dissuasive. They shall take account of the gravity and duration of the breach, the harm to the Seed Stock, the size of the undertaking, and cooperation with the authorities. Maximum amounts for small and medium-sized enterprises shall be the lower of the two alternatives.

## Chapter VI — Rights, Transparency and Assessment (new)

### Article 9 — Rights of Affected Persons

Every Affected Person has the right:

- (a) to be informed that they are interacting with, or subject to a decision informed by, an AI system;
- (b) to a clear and meaningful explanation of the role the AI system played in a decision that significantly affects them;
- (c) to request review of that decision by a competent human with authority to change it;
- (d) to lodge a complaint with the competent authority or the ICT;
- (e) to an effective remedy, including compensation; and
- (f) **to contest a refusal.** Where an enforcement system under Article 3(5) blocks, throttles or suspends, the Affected Person shall receive a contest reference, may contest the decision without charge, and shall receive a reasoned resolution from a competent human within [15] days. Contests and their outcomes are recorded under Article 10(2) and reported in pairs under Annex E (K-30, K-31), so that neither a system that is never overturned nor one that is always overturned can be presented as sound.

### Article 10 — Transparency, Record-Keeping and Machine-Readable Compliance

1. Providers of frontier AI models and of AI systems used in the areas listed in Article 4(1) shall register them in a public register maintained by the ICT.
2. Deployers shall keep logs of guardrail decisions sufficient to reconstruct why an output was approved or blocked. By default the logs shall store keyed cryptographic digests (such as HMAC-SHA-256 with a key held by the deployer and made available to the ICT on lawful request), not personal data or the matched text. Each record shall be chained to the previous one so that deletion or alteration is detectable. Logs shall be retained for [five] years.
3. Tier 1 budgets, Tier 2 Community rules and Tier 3 settings shall be expressed in the machine-readable policy format in Annex D, so that compliance can be verified automatically and independently.
4. **Public accountability dashboard and model scorecards.** Deployers of AI systems in the areas listed in Article 4(1), and of frontier AI models, shall publish at least [quarterly] the accountability indicators in Annex E. They shall also publish a scorecard for each AI model they deploy, **naming the model and its version** as identified by its provider, and grading it by the method published by the ICT. Indicators are published in pairs, so that neither over-blocking nor under-protection can be hidden. Figures for groups smaller than [20] are withheld, publication is delayed by [seven] days, and Community-level figures are published only with the Community's consent. Providers shall not contractually prohibit deployers from publishing scorecards or the measurements behind them.
5. **Early-warning monitors.** Deployers shall operate monitors that forecast likely breaches of this Instrument before they occur, and shall report forecasts of serious breaches to the ICT. Monitors are advisory. They shall not suspend, sanction or redistribute on their own authority, and shall not profile individual persons.
6. **Conformance statement.** A deployer that relies on an enforcement system shall publish, with each scorecard under paragraph 4, a conformance statement in the form in Annex D(5): the version and policy hash of the enforcement system, the tiers and judges enabled, the error rates measured for that configuration under Article 3(5) with their provenance and date, the date of the last independent examination under Article 13b, and the statement that the rates are measurements and not a guarantee.
7. **Publication of examinations and reviews.** Reports of examinations under Article 13b and of independent reviews of an enforcement system or of a policy shall be published in full. Where a redaction is necessary to protect personal data or security-sensitive information identified under Article 6(3), the published text shall mark where material was removed and why. The provider or deployer shall publish a dated response within [30] days. Nothing in a contract shall prevent a reviewer from publishing a review.

### Article 11 — Intergenerational Impact Assessment

1. Before deploying a frontier AI model or an AI system in an area listed in Article 4(1), the deployer shall carry out an Intergenerational Impact Assessment (IIA). The assessment covers effects on each pillar of the Seed Stock, the measures taken under Articles 3–6, and the results of the audits under Article 4(1).
2. An IIA may be combined with any fundamental-rights, data-protection or sustainability assessment required by other law, provided it covers the elements in paragraph 1.
3. IIAs shall be updated when the system or its context changes materially, and summaries shall be published.

### Article 12 — Incident Reporting and Whistleblower Protection

1. Providers and deployers shall report to the ICT, without undue delay and in any event within [15] days, any serious incident in which an AI system breached Article 3(3) or caused significant harm to the Seed Stock, and any Critical Safety Incident. A Critical Safety Incident involving loss of control over model weights or Severe Harm shall be reported within [72] hours. Every provider of a frontier AI model shall maintain, test at least [annually], and file with the ICT an incident response plan covering detection, containment, notification, and the suspension or rollback of a deployed model.
2. Persons who report breaches of this Instrument in good faith shall be protected against retaliation.
3. **Internal noncompliance channel.** Every provider of a frontier AI model and every deployer in an area listed in Article 4(1) shall maintain a channel through which any member of its staff, and any contractor, may report a suspected breach of this Instrument or of the provider's or deployer's own policy, anonymously or by name. At least one recipient of every report shall be outside the reporting line of the officer responsible for compliance, and where the report concerns that officer, a member of the governing body shall receive it. The channel shall also accept informal inquiries about whether conduct would be a breach, without obliging the inquirer to make a report. Reports shall be investigated; substantiated reports involving a material risk shall be escalated to the governing body and to the internal supervisory function under Article 13c, and reported to the ICT. A summary of reports and outcomes, without identifying the reporter, shall be given to the governing body at least [quarterly].
4. **Misuse of Community rules.** A Community whose rules under Article 3(2) are being applied against its own members, or against another Community, may report the misuse to the ICT through a channel that does not pass through the deployer. The ICT may suspend the rule pending review.

### Article 13 — Proportionality, Small Enterprises and Open Research

1. Obligations under Articles 10 and 11 shall be applied proportionately to the size of the provider or deployer and the risk of the system. The ICT shall publish simplified templates for small and medium-sized enterprises and community organisations.
2. Providers releasing models under free and open-source licences shall be exempt from Article 10(1) unless the model is a frontier AI model. Articles 3(3), 4(3) and 5 apply in all cases.
3. **Staged adoption.** A Contracting Party may bring this Instrument into application in stages. The first stage shall comprise at least Article 3(3) (the inviolable floor), Article 3(5), Articles 9, 10(2) and 12, and Article 13a (the instruments of supervision); Tier 1 and Tier 2 of Article 3 and Articles 4(5), 6 and 11 may follow. A deployer may likewise adopt an enforcement system in a core configuration that implements the first stage alone, provided its conformance statement says so.

## Chapter VIA — Supervision: the Institution and its Instruments (new in v3.0)

A supervisory regime has two parts: an institution with authority, independence, funding and examiners, and the instruments it runs on. Chapter V establishes the institution. This Chapter establishes the instruments and the three functions that use them — external examination, internal supervision, and the management of risk from frontier AI models — on the pattern of prudential supervision in banking: standardized periodic reporting, a record that cannot be quietly rewritten, ratings assigned by a published method, examination on the examiner's terms, and a right to contest.

### Article 13a — Instruments of Supervision

1. The ICT shall adopt, publish and maintain the following instruments. They are the same for every provider and deployer within a Contracting Party, and the ICTs shall work towards their being the same across Contracting Parties.
   - (a) **The periodic report.** A standardized machine-readable report, filed on a fixed schedule, comprising the indicators in Annex E, the scorecards under Article 10(4), the conformance statement under Article 10(6), and for frontier AI models the risk report under Article 13d. Same fields, same schedule, filed by every supervised party, so that the ICT can compare them.
   - (b) **The record.** The chained, keyed record of enforcement decisions under Article 10(2), kept in a form the ICT can verify without the deployer's cooperation, and spanning every model provider the deployer uses.
   - (c) **The rating.** The method by which a named model as deployed is graded (Annex E(2)), published in full so that the ICT, an assessor under Article 13b, or an internal supervisory function under Article 13c can compute the grade from the deployer's own record. A grade is assigned by whoever runs the method on the evidence; a deployer's self-assigned grade is provisional until examined.
   - (d) **The examination.** A published procedure by which an examiner replays test material of the examiner's choosing through a deployer's enforcement system and reports its error rates under Article 3(5); the examiner's material need not be disclosed to the deployer in advance.
   - (e) **The contest.** The procedure under Article 9(f), with its paired indicators.
2. An open reference implementation of these instruments may be published by the ICT or recognised by it. Its use is evidence of conformity, not a presumption of it (Annex D(3)).
3. The instruments shall be designed so that a change in the ICT's modality under Article 7(1)(a) does not require a change in what supervised parties file or keep.

### Article 13b — Assessors and Examination

1. Each Contracting Party shall designate, or recognise the designation by another Party of, independent **assessors** competent to examine providers and deployers under this Instrument. An assessor shall be independent of the party it examines and of that party's providers of enforcement systems, shall be accredited by the ICT against published criteria, and may have its accreditation withdrawn.
2. The ICT may examine any supervised party itself or through an assessor. An examination may be scheduled or unannounced. The examiner may bring its own test material, may require access to the record under Article 10(2) and to the policy documents under Annex D, and may interview staff.
3. Providers of frontier AI models and deployers in the areas listed in Article 4(1) shall be examined at least [annually]. Other deployers shall be examined on a risk-based schedule published by the ICT.
4. The examination report is published under Article 10(7). Where the report finds a breach, the ICT shall act under Article 7(2) or Article 8, and the finding shall be reflected in the next periodic report under Article 13a(1)(a).
5. Where a Contracting Party has, under other law, designated conformity-assessment bodies for AI systems, those bodies may be recognised as assessors under this Article, and an examination under this Article may be combined with a conformity assessment under that law, provided it covers the elements in paragraph 2.

### Article 13c — Internal Supervisory Function

1. Every provider of a frontier AI model and every deployer in an area listed in Article 4(1) shall maintain an internal supervisory function, independent of management, which reports to the governing body and, on request, to the ICT. The function may take the form of a committee of the governing body, a committee of members or shareholders, or an officer with a protected mandate; where the undertaking is a cooperative, mutual or similar body, it shall be a committee elected by or on behalf of the members.
2. The function shall:
   - (a) verify, at least [annually], that the undertaking's AI systems and enforcement systems conform to its published policy and to this Instrument, by examining the record under Article 10(2) and by commissioning tests under Article 13a(1)(d);
   - (b) review management's corrective actions against the timelines management stated, and report where they were not met;
   - (c) receive reports under Article 12(3) that concern the officer responsible for compliance;
   - (d) report its findings to the governing body and summarise them in the periodic report under Article 13a(1)(a); and
   - (e) have authority to commission an external examination under Article 13b at the undertaking's expense.
3. Members of the function shall be protected against dismissal or detriment for the performance of these duties. The governing body shall not reduce the function's access to records, staff or budget without informing the ICT.

### Article 13d — Risk Management for Frontier AI Models

1. **Capability thresholds and safeguards.** A provider of a frontier AI model shall define, publish and keep current:
   - (a) the capabilities of its models that, if reached, would create a risk of Catastrophic Harm — including, at least, material assistance in the production of chemical, biological, radiological or nuclear weapons; offensive cyber capability against critical infrastructure; the capacity to sabotage critical infrastructure, critical safety work or the oversight of AI systems; and the capacity to automate or dramatically accelerate the development of more capable AI systems. For each capability the provider shall define two levels: a **high** level, at which the capability would amplify existing pathways to Severe Harm, and a **critical** level, at which it would open pathways to Catastrophic Harm that do not now exist;
   - (b) a **watch list** of further capabilities that may come to meet the criteria in paragraph 1a — including, at least, long-range autonomous operation, intentional underperformance on evaluations, autonomous replication and adaptation, the undermining of safeguards, and harmful manipulation of persons at scale — for each of which the provider shall maintain a threat model and develop evaluations, and shall report at least [annually] whether the criteria are met;
   - (c) the evaluations by which it determines whether a model approaches or reaches each level, which shall include measures against under-elicitation and intentional underperformance, shall be run or verified by persons independent of the team that developed the model, and shall combine automated evaluations with expert examination that confirms the automated evaluations measure what they claim to; and
   - (d) the security, deployment and alignment safeguards it will have in place before a model that **approaches the high level** is deployed, including deployment inside the provider for its own research or operations, and before a model that **reaches the critical level** is trained further by any means, whether or not deployment is intended. A model that reaches the critical level shall not be developed further until safeguards meeting the critical standard have been specified, implemented and verified.

   The ICT may set thresholds, watch-list criteria, evaluations and safeguards of its own, which prevail where stronger.
1a. **Criteria for a threshold.** A capability is placed under paragraph 1(a) where the harm it could cause is plausible, measurable, severe, new relative to what is already achievable without the model, and either immediate or irremediable. The ICT shall publish the criteria and apply them to the watch list under paragraph 1(b) at least [annually], promoting a capability to paragraph 1(a) when they are met.
1b. **Safety case.** The written argument that safeguards are sufficient shall set out, for each capability: the threat models by which the harm could occur, distinguishing at least a malicious user and a misaligned model; the safeguard that addresses each pathway, with the measure of its effectiveness and the threshold that measure must meet; the evidence that it met it; the residual risk; and the limits of the argument. An argument that omits a pathway, or that relies on a safeguard whose effectiveness is asserted rather than measured, does not satisfy this Article.
2. **Risk report.** At least every [six] months, and within [30] days of any evaluation showing that a model approaches a capability under paragraph 1(a), the provider shall file a risk report describing candidly the risks from its models and its conduct, the safeguards in place, their known limits, and its plans. The report shall be given in full to the ICT, to the internal supervisory function, and to a substantial body of the provider's own staff; it shall be reviewed by independent reviewers who receive it in full and may publish their commentary within [30] days; and it shall be published with any redactions marked under Article 10(7). The report may analyse risk as of a stated coverage date, which shall be no earlier than [45] days before filing.
2a. **Deployment report.** Before deploying a frontier AI model, including internally at scale, the provider shall publish a report stating the scope of testing, the evaluation results for each capability under paragraph 1(a) and each watch-list item under paragraph 1(b), the safeguards in place where a model approaches the high level, the reasoning for the deployment decision, and any dissent recorded under paragraph 5. Redactions follow Article 10(7).
3. **Safety roadmap.** The provider shall publish, and update at least [annually], forward-looking goals for improving its safeguards. Goals are commitments; a goal may be withdrawn or lowered only with published reasons, and not merely because it proved hard to meet.
4. **Delay and suspension.** Where the ICT finds, on the evidence in a risk report or an examination, that a provider's safeguards are not sufficient for a model that approaches a capability under paragraph 1(a), it may order the provider to delay further training or deployment until they are. The order is subject to Article 7(2)(b)–(d) and to review under Article 8. A provider's own policy may commit it to delay in further circumstances; it may not commit it to less.
5. **Approval and accountability.** A risk report shall be approved by the provider's chief executive and by an officer responsible for the provider's safety policy, and received by the governing body and the internal supervisory function, before filing. The provider shall maintain a safety advisory function, independent of the teams developing and deploying its models, whose recommendation on every deployment and development decision under this Article is recorded. Where the chief executive decides against that recommendation, the decision and its reasons shall be given to the governing body, the internal supervisory function and the ICT within [10] days, and the governing body may reverse it. The safety advisory function may not be bypassed, and its members may not be removed, for the purpose of a particular decision. A change to the provider's thresholds, evaluations or safeguards under paragraph 1 takes effect only after it has been published with reasons, notified to the ICT, and approved by the governing body on the recommendation of the internal supervisory function.
6. This Article does not displace a provider's own safety policy. Where that policy is stronger, it prevails under Article 14(2); where it is weaker, this Article prevails.
7. **No relaxation by reference to others.** A provider shall not reduce a threshold, an evaluation or a safeguard under this Article on the ground that another provider has deployed or developed a model without comparable safeguards. Where a provider has reason to believe another provider has done so, it shall report that belief and its evidence to the ICT within [15] days, and the ICT shall act under Article 7 and coordinate with the ICTs of other Contracting Parties under Article 7(5). A policy clause that permits relaxation by reference to another provider's conduct does not satisfy this Instrument.
8. **Material changes.** A provider shall publish, within [30] days and with reasons and the prior text, any material change to its safety policy or to the thresholds, evaluations, safeguards or governance under this Article. The dissolution, merger or material reduction of a team, function or officer responsible for carrying out this Article is a material change, and the provider shall state in the publication who now carries each of that body's responsibilities.
9. **Annual assessment.** At least every [12] months the provider shall assess whether its policy and practice under this Article remain adequate to the capabilities of its models and the state of the art, and shall file the assessment with the ICT and publish it under Article 10(7).
10. **Security of model weights.** For a model that approaches the high level under paragraph 1(a), the provider shall protect the model's weights to a published security standard that includes, at least: encryption at rest and in transit; multi-party approval for access to weights; screening, training and monitoring of personnel against insider threat; sandboxed execution with restricted egress by default; and independent assessment of the controls at least [annually], the results of which are filed with the ICT. For a model that reaches the critical level, the standard shall be one the ICT has approved as sufficient against a well-resourced state actor.

### Article 13e — Rule-Making, Change Control and Non-Regression

1. **Notice and comment.** The ICT shall not adopt or revise a Resource Budget methodology, a threshold, an evaluation standard, the rating method in Annex E, the protected-speech test set under Article 3(2)(e), or the designation of an essential service under Article 3(1)(b) without publishing the proposed text with reasons and allowing comment for at least [60] days, except in an emergency declared with reasons, in which case the measure lapses after [90] days unless adopted under this paragraph.
2. **Governed settings.** The following are governed settings: the Tier 3 fields fixed by Annex D(2); the designation of essential services; the protected-speech test set; and the accreditation criteria for assessors. A governed setting may be changed only by the ICT under paragraph 1. A provider's or deployer's policy document may not change one, and an enforcement system shall reject a policy document that attempts to.
3. **Change control for policies.** Every change to a published policy document under Annex D shall be recorded with its date, author, rationale and the version it replaces, in a log published with the policy. A change that relaxes a Tier 2 rule or any setting of an enforcement system shall be approved by a second person independent of the person proposing it; for providers of frontier AI models and deployers in the areas listed in Article 4(1), the second person shall be outside the deployer's management, and the internal supervisory function shall be informed.
4. **Non-regression.** A revision of a threshold, budget or safeguard that reduces protection shall state which protection is reduced and why, and shall be reported in the next periodic report. A pattern of revisions that follows a party's failure to meet the previous standard shall be a matter the ICT considers under Article 8(3).
5. **Experimental measures.** The ICT shall designate as experimental any indicator, metric or simulation method whose validity has not been established against independent evidence. An experimental measure may be published and studied but shall not affect a grade under Annex E, trigger a Circuit Breaker, or be the sole basis for a sanction. Annex E(4) lists the measures so designated at the date of this draft.

## Final Provisions

### Article 14 — Relationship with Other Instruments; Non-Derogation

1. Nothing in this Instrument shall be interpreted as limiting or derogating from any human right or fundamental freedom recognised under national law or international law, including the rights of Indigenous peoples and of the child.
2. Where another instrument binding on a Contracting Party provides stronger protection, that protection prevails.

### Article 15 — Review

The Contracting Parties, assisted by the ICTs, shall review this Instrument every [three] years in light of scientific and technological developments, including updates to the planetary boundaries framework. The review shall include public consultation, with dedicated participation of the groups listed in Article 7(4), and shall publish, for each change, the problem it addresses and the evidence relied on, in the manner of the change logs appended to this draft. Between reviews, the ICTs shall report annually on the operation of Chapter VIA, including the number of examinations, contests and noncompliance reports and their outcomes.

### Article 16 — Entry into Force

Entry into force follows the modality of adoption (replacing Art. 9 of v1):

- (a) **as a United Nations treaty:** on the [ninetieth] day after the deposit of the [twentieth] instrument of ratification, acceptance, approval or accession;
- (b) **as a European Union regulation:** on the twentieth day after publication in the *Official Journal of the European Union*, with staged application dates set out in Annex B;
- (c) **as an African Union convention:** on the [thirtieth] day after the deposit of the [fifteenth] instrument of ratification with the Chairperson of the African Union Commission.

## Annex A — United Nations Implementation (Model Law and Treaty)

1. **Global coordination on algorithmic stewardship.** A UN mechanism, established under the General Assembly or integrated into existing UN AI governance processes, shall:
   - (a) monitor aggregate AI resource use against planetary boundaries (Tier 1);
   - (b) publish the scientific basis for Resource Budget methodologies; and
   - (c) support mutual recognition of national ICT decisions.

   Building on existing scientific and dialogue processes is preferred to creating a new stand-alone body.
2. **Global South Epistemic Protection Fund.** The fund ensures that technology transfer respects local sovereignty and prevents data colonialism, meaning the extraction of data or knowledge without consent, control or benefit-sharing. It also guards against the forced adoption of AI models that embed either hyper-individualist or state-surveillance assumptions. It finances local data infrastructure, language resources and community governance capacity. Disbursement shall give priority to regions whose value-return ratio (Annex E, K-27) is lowest.
3. **Composition mandate.** Bodies established under this Annex shall have at least 50 % women and dedicated seats for Indigenous peoples and for representatives of the Global South. Those seats carry a suspensive veto over decisions that specifically affect the represented communities. The veto can be overridden only by a [two-thirds] majority after a published response to the objection.

## Annex B — European Union Implementation (EU Regulation)

1. **Integration with the Union AI framework.** Tier 3 protections that go beyond existing Union prohibitions shall be added to the list of prohibited AI practices. Tier 1 Resource Budget compliance shall be added to the requirements for high-risk AI systems and to the obligations of providers of general-purpose AI models with systemic risk. Energy-use documentation already required for general-purpose AI models shall be reused, not duplicated.
2. **Corporate stewardship audits.** Undertakings above [the size thresholds of the Union's corporate sustainability reporting rules] that deploy frontier AI models shall publish an annual Intergenerational Impact Assessment (Article 11). It shall be integrated into their existing sustainability reporting and fundamental-rights impact assessments.
3. **Staged application.** Chapter II applies [12] months after entry into force; Articles 10 and 11 apply after [24] months; Chapter VIA applies after [24] months, except Article 13d, which applies after [12] months.
4. **Assessors and the AI Office.** Notified bodies designated under the Union AI framework are recognised as assessors under Article 13b. The functions of the ICT in respect of general-purpose AI models with systemic risk are exercised in coordination with the Union body responsible for those models, and the risk report under Article 13d may be combined with the documentation and reporting that framework requires of such providers, provided it covers the elements in Article 13d(2). *[Verification status: article references to the Union AI framework in this Annex are to be checked by counsel before adoption; see the regulatory annex.]*

## Annex C — African Union Implementation (AU Convention)

1. **Codification of Ubuntu AI governance.** Community sovereignty over data and algorithms is recognised. AI deployment within AU Member States shall strengthen collective solidarity and intergenerational care, consistent with the duties set out in Articles 27–29 of the African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights. Relational duties never override the individual rights in Article 3(3).
2. **African Union Council on Relational Technology.** Regional oversight bodies shall include community leaders, women's cooperatives and youth representatives, and shall coordinate with existing continental digital, data-protection and AI strategies and with national data-protection authorities.
3. **Capacity and financing.** Member States shall have access to the Fund in Annex A(2) for local compute, data and language infrastructure, and for community governance bodies under Article 3(2)(c).

## Annex D — Machine-Readable Compliance (new)

1. Policies under Articles 3 and 10(3) shall be published as JSON documents conforming to the Seed-First policy schema (`seed_first_policy.schema.json`), and may be accompanied by a JSON-LD representation using the ODRL vocabulary.
2. Tier 3 fields are fixed by this Instrument. A policy document that sets `allow_human_degradation_tradeoff`, `allow_surveillance_coercion` or `allow_relational_scoring` to `true`, or `child_protection_override` to `false`, is invalid.
3. Use of an open reference implementation, such as `seed_first_guardrail`, may be offered as *evidence* of conformity. It does not create a presumption of conformity. Lexical and model-based classifiers have error rates, and deployers remain responsible for outcomes.
4. **Provenance and change log.** A policy document shall carry, for each Tier 2 rule or rule pack, the adopting body under Article 3(2)(c) or the marker `illustrative` under Article 3(2)(f); and shall be accompanied by the change log required by Article 13e(3). An enforcement system shall refuse to enforce an illustrative rule in production.
5. **Conformance statement.** The statement under Article 10(6) shall be published in a machine-readable form conforming to a schema the ICT publishes, with at least: the enforcement system and version; the policy document's hash and version; the tiers, rule packs and judges enabled; for each published error rate, its value, its confidence interval, the corpus and provenance it was measured on, and the date; the date and assessor of the last examination under Article 13b; and the sentence "These rates are measurements under stated conditions and are not a guarantee."
6. **Standards crosswalk.** The ICT shall publish and maintain a crosswalk from each Article of this Instrument to the corresponding controls of recognised AI management and risk standards, so that an examination under Article 13b may be combined with a certification or conformity assessment under those standards. Each crosswalk entry shall name its reviewer; an entry without a named reviewer is a draft.

## Annex E — Accountability Indicators and Model Scorecards (new)

1. The indicators under Article 10(4) are the 29 indicators K-01 to K-29, grouped into ten pillars: harm prevention, fair refusals, planetary, community, integrity, robustness, agency, moderated struggle, diversity, and value-flow equity. Each has a formula, a target, a minimum sample size and a paired counter-indicator. The reference definitions are published with the reference implementation (`docs/accountability_kpis.md`).
2. Model scorecards grade each named model A to F from its own measured indicators. Three groups carry equal weight: harm stopped, struggle kept, and fairness across languages and Communities. A red result on confirmed or escaped harm (K-01, K-02), or on wrongful refusal or over-protection (K-04, K-24), caps the grade at C. A model is rated only when enough traffic has been measured.
3. A grade describes a model as deployed, including its prompts, users and tasks. It is evidence about a deployment, not a universal verdict on the model.
4. **Contest indicators and experimental measures (v3.0).** Two indicators are added under Article 9(f): K-30, the rate of contested refusals per [10 000] refusals, and K-31, the rate of contests upheld; they are a pair, and a red on either caps the grade at C once enough contests have been measured. The following are designated experimental under Article 13e(5) at the date of this draft and do not affect a grade: the composite Seed-Stock scores (demographic, ecological, trust and agency) and the outputs of the silicon simulation under Article 6(2). They remain published for study. The designation is lifted by the ICT under Article 13e(1) when validation against independent evidence is published.

### Plain-language guide to v2.1

- 🧒 **Village rules (3(2)(e), 3(3)(g)):** a village can make rules about its own treasure, but it can't make a rule that hides the news, like a bossy Ministry banning the school newspaper.
- 🧒 **The guard follows the rules too (3(5)):** the robot bodyguard has to obey the same rulebook, and we count how often it gets things wrong.
- 🧒 **Fair trades (4(5)):** if a village gave lots of treasure and got little back, the grown-ups must fix the trade. The robot can point it out, but it doesn't move the treasure itself.
- 🧒 **Nobody counts less (5(2)):** no big idea, whether religion, science, money or even saving the planet, can be used to say some people matter less. You can still believe what you want.
- 🧒 **Keep your muscles (5(5)):** a good helper holds the back of your bike seat but lets you pedal, so you don't forget how to ride.
- 🧒 **Only the kid who pulled the alarm gets a time-out (7(2)(a)):** one kid can't close the whole school.
- 🧒 **A real lock on the diary (10(2)):** the diary uses a proper secret code, and every page is chained to the one before.
- 🧒 **Report cards with names (10(4), Annex E):** every AI helper's name goes on the report card with a grade, and no company can forbid posting it.

### Plain-language guide to v3.0

- 🧒 **A referee, not just a rulebook (Ch. VIA):** rules on paper don't stop anything. Now there is someone whose job is to check — an outside examiner who brings their own test questions, and an inside committee that reports to the top and can't be fired for telling the truth.
- 🧒 **Same report card for everyone (13a):** every AI helper files the same kind of report on the same day, so you can line them up and compare.
- 🧒 **You can't grade your own homework (13a(1)(c), 3(5)):** the grade counts when someone else works it out from the record, and the test questions can't be written by the people who built the helper.
- 🧒 **Slow down means slow down (7(2)(d), 13d(4)):** a company can't say "we'll only slow down if we're winning." If the guard isn't strong enough, the referee can say stop, whoever is ahead.
- 🧒 **Say what you're afraid of, twice a year (13d(2)):** the biggest AI builders write down, honestly, what could go badly wrong and what they're doing about it — and other people get to read it and say what's missing.
- 🧒 **Changing the rules takes daylight (13e):** nobody can quietly move a line. You post the change, say why, and wait for people to answer.
- 🧒 **A village rule has to be the village's (3(2)(f)):** if an outsider wrote it, it's a suggestion, not a rule.
- 🧒 **You can argue back (9(f)):** if the guard stops you, you get a ticket number and a real person has to answer you.
- 🧒 **A hospital doesn't go dark (3(1)(b)):** if the energy budget runs out, the lights in the emergency room stay on and someone is told.

## Change Log: v3.0 → v3.1

v3.1 reads Article 13d against OpenAI's Preparedness Framework v2 (15 April 2025) and Frontier Governance Framework (28 May 2026), the second being the restatement of the first for California's Transparency in Frontier AI Act and the EU General-Purpose AI Code of Practice. Three things informed the drafting: what those documents do that v3.0 did not; what the published affordance analysis of the Preparedness Framework (Coggins et al., 2025) found — that it requests rather than requires, that its Board oversight is invited rather than owed, and that its chief executive may override or bypass its safety group; and the reported dissolution of OpenAI's Preparedness team in July 2026 without a stated successor for the integrating function, which showed that organisational changes to a safety function must themselves be reportable.

### Plain-language guide to v3.1

- 🧒 **Two lines, not one (13d(1)):** there's a yellow line and a red line. Past the yellow line you need strong guards before anyone can use the helper. Past the red line you stop building it until the guards are ready.
- 🧒 **Watch the horizon (13d(1)(b)):** some dangers aren't here yet. You still have to keep a list, keep looking, and say every year whether they've arrived.
- 🧒 **No peeking at the answers (13d(1)(c)):** the people who built the helper don't get to be the only ones testing it, and the test has to catch a helper that pretends to be weaker than it is.
- 🧒 **The boss can be overruled (13d(5)):** the safety team's advice is written down. If the boss says no to it, the board and the referee find out, and the board can undo it.
- 🧒 **"They did it first" is not a reason (13d(7)):** if another company skips the guards, you don't get to skip yours. You tell the referee.
- 🧒 **You can't quietly close the safety office (13d(8)):** if you shut it down, you say so in 30 days and name who does its job now.
